# The Aphelion Deep Sensory Vocabulary

Consolidated from the supplied Aphelion Deep design sources. Working design; unresolved passages retain their original status. The tutorial amendments in Core Rules govern the declared three turn slice.

# Abyssfall — Sensory Vocabulary
*Reference document for card writers, facilitators, and anyone writing text that describes this world*

---

> *The ocean does not announce itself. It simply becomes more present the deeper you go, until presence is all there is.*

---

## How to use this document

This is not a lore document. It is a writing tool.

Before writing any card text, read-aloud line, or event description set at a specific tier, check the relevant section here. The goal is not uniformity — cards should have distinct voices — but coherence. A player who has been reading Tier 1 cards for two hours should feel the difference when they draw their first Tier 3 card, without anyone explaining that the world has changed.

Every tier has four registers: **light**, **sound**, **body** (pressure, temperature, physical sensation), and **biology** (what the living things are doing). Write to at least two of these per card that describes a place. The deeper the tier, the more the registers should feel like they are coming from the same source.

The Depth has a sensory signature that runs from Tier 3 to Tier 5. It builds. By the time players reach Tier 5, it should feel like something they have been noticing for a long time — not a reveal, but a recognition.

## Tier 1 — The Sunlit Shallows

### The experience

Tier 1 is the most legible part of this world. It is genuinely beautiful in a way that is almost disarming — players arriving here after the crash should feel the world extending an unearned welcome, which makes what comes later more pointed.

The light here is warm and abundant, filtering through shallow water in shifting columns. It moves constantly. The visual effect is less like being underwater and more like being inside light itself — everything touched by it, nothing quite still.

### Light

Amber-gold in the upper shallows, cooling to pale green at the shelf edge. Visibility is high — thirty metres on a clear turn, less when the tidal currents stir the organic matter. Shadows are soft and multiple; the light refracts around organisms and formations, creating duplicates. Things look slightly doubled at the edges.

At night, or in the shadow of the wreck: the bioluminescence here is incidental, decorative almost — individual organisms pulsing at their own rhythms, uncoordinated, like a city seen from above.

*Writing signal: Tier 1 light is generous. It shows you things. Use it to make the world feel accessible.*

### Sound

Busy. The Tier 1 acoustic environment is the richest in the game — clicks, pulses, the low percussion of shells and formations settling, the higher frequencies of small fauna in motion. It reads as noise before it reads as signal.

Underneath all of it, at the very bottom of the audible range, there is a tone. It has always been there. Nobody notices it at Tier 1 because there is too much else to hear.

*Writing signal: mention the noise. The tone beneath it is not yet worth naming.*

### Body

Warm. The water at Tier 1 is the closest this ocean gets to comfortable — not tropical, but manageable without full thermal protection. Current is present but gentle, mostly tidal, predictable. The pressure is almost nothing.

Physical effort here is metabolic, not mechanical. The body works to stay warm and oxygenated; the equipment works to filter and recycle. The main physical experience is the weight of the water itself — not hostile, just constant, a reminder that this is not air.

*Writing signal: Tier 1 physical discomfort is mild and familiar. Save the body's attention for deeper tiers.*

### Biology

Everything is moving. The ecology at Tier 1 is dense and active — grazing fauna on the reef formations, filter feeders in the current, small predators cycling through the Flora in patterns that look, with enough observation, almost choreographed.

The organisms here are not obviously alien. They have analogues — things that swim like fish, things that wave like anemones, things that cluster like coral. The alienness is in the details: the wrong number of appendages, the symmetry that isn't quite bilateral, the colours that don't match any prey-warning or camouflage logic you were taught. You notice it, and then you stop noticing it, which is its own kind of strangeness.

*Writing signal: Tier 1 biology is abundant and almost-familiar. The alienness is quiet, detail-level. It should not be the first thing mentioned.*

## Tier 2 — The Kelp and Ruins

### The experience

Tier 2 is where the world gets its scale. The kelp forests here are not background — they are architecture, vertical and massive, creating corridors and chambers and depths-within-depths. Players stop moving across the world and start moving through it.

The ruins appear here for the first time. They are almost the right shape. That is what is wrong with them.

### Light

The warm amber of the shallows is gone. Light at Tier 2 is filtered through the kelp canopy — green-blue, directional, arriving in shafts rather than columns. It illuminates vertically, which means the floors of kelp corridors are dim and the mid-water is bright and the canopy is the brightest thing in the world. Movement in the canopy creates moving light on everything below it.

In the ruins and the Broken Trenchline: the light is blocked. Pools of near-darkness in structures that were clearly designed for senses that did not depend on it.

*Writing signal: Tier 2 light is conditional. It reveals some things and not others, and the player does not always control which.*

### Sound

Quieter than Tier 1, but more structured. The biological noise drops off as the ecosystem changes composition. What remains: the low creak of kelp in current, the percussion of formations, the occasional distant impact from something large moving in the mid-water.

And the tone. Still at the bottom of the range, but louder here — or rather, more audible, because the noise above it has thinned. It is not a natural sound. Instruments reading it for the first time will flag it as signal rather than ambient. It does not match any known frequency band. It has been here a long time.

*Writing signal: name the tone at Tier 2. Not as a revelation — as a data point. Something to log and move past. Players who pay attention will return to it.*

### Body

Colder. The thermocline between Tier 1 and Tier 2 is the first physical threshold — a drop of several degrees that the body registers before the instruments confirm it. Thermal protection becomes necessary rather than optional.

Current is more complex here: the tidal patterns that governed Tier 1 interact with the kelp's own resistance, creating local eddies and backflows. Navigation by feel becomes unreliable. Equipment handles it; the body just notes that the water is less predictable than it was.

The ruins, when you enter them: stale. The water inside has been separated from the main circulation long enough to develop its own chemistry. Not dangerous, not yet, but different in a way that the body registers as wrong.

*Writing signal: Tier 2 introduces cold and complexity. The body is working harder without being in danger yet.*

### Biology

Less abundant than Tier 1, more specialised. The organisms here have adapted to the vertical environment — flatter profiles, better low-light sensitivity, different feeding strategies. Predators at Tier 2 are more visible because they move between the lit mid-water and the dark floor, crossing the light rather than hiding from it.

The kelp itself. This is not a plant in any sense you were trained for. It does not have roots in the conventional sense; it anchors to the mineral formations below and feeds on the current above. Its growth patterns are fractal and, at sufficient scale, create structures that look like they were planned. Corridors that run straight for thirty metres before turning at exactly the same angle. Chambers where multiple kelp organisms have grown together into something that functions like a ceiling. You remind yourself it is not deliberate.

*Writing signal: Tier 2 biology is adapted and efficient. The organisms are more purposeful-looking. The kelp specifically should feel like it is making decisions it isn't making.*

### The ruins — Tier 2 first appearance

The Drowned structures at Tier 2 are partial — grown over, partially collapsed, integrated into the kelp ecosystem in ways that make them difficult to distinguish from natural formations at a distance.

The tell is scale and angle. Natural formations at this depth have the geometry of accumulation — irregular, weighted toward the base, following the path of least resistance. Drowned structures have the geometry of intention: corridors that continue past the point where a natural passage would have closed, chambers with walls that meet at angles geology does not produce, openings at heights that imply the body that used them was a specific size.

Up close, the material is wrong. Not wrong like an alien mineral — wrong like something that was grown according to a plan. The substrate is biological but structured, layered in a way that organic growth does not produce unassisted. It is the difference between a coral formation and a coral formation that someone helped.

No inscriptions here. No records. Just structure, and the fact of it.

*Writing signal: first contact with the ruins is sensory, not informational. They are wrong in a way that takes a moment to name. Do not explain what they are. Describe what is strange about them.*

## Tier 3 — The Twilight Shelf

### The experience

Tier 3 is where the world stops being almost-familiar. The shift is not dramatic — there is no single moment — but by the time a player has read several Tier 3 cards, they should feel that they are somewhere genuinely different. The biology is stranger. The light is doing something the light in Tiers 1 and 2 was not doing. The Depth is present for the first time in a way that can be felt rather than measured.

### Light

Solar light is gone. What remains is entirely biological — the bioluminescence of the organisms at this depth, which is not incidental or decorative but structural. The organisms here produce light as a primary sense: navigation, communication, predation, display. The result is an environment that is lit from within and from many directions simultaneously, with no single source.

The bioluminescence at Tier 3 is blue-green in dominant frequency, with pulses of violet and, rarely, amber. Individual organisms have their own frequencies. Schools of fauna produce interference patterns when they move together — bands of light and dark that travel through the water like a visible sound.

And the patterns are not random. Not obviously coordinated, not consciously produced — but not random. A biologist paying attention will notice, sometime in the second or third expedition to a Tier 3 location, that the light pulses are not independent. Something is influencing the phase. Whatever it is, it is operating below the threshold of any individual organism's awareness.

*Writing signal: Tier 3 light is alive and patterned. It comes from the biology, not from above. Use it to light scenes from below and from the sides, never from above. The patterns should be noticeable without being explicable.*

### Sound

The biological noise has dropped further. Tier 3 is acoustically spare — the fauna here are larger, slower, more widely spaced. What sound there is carries further than it should.

The tone is louder. It is no longer a data point to be logged and set aside. It sits at the lower edge of what the body can feel as vibration rather than hear as sound — a pressure in the chest, a resonance in the bones of the hands that are holding the instruments. Instruments confirm it as signal. The frequency is consistent. It does not drift or pulse. It has been broadcasting, at the same frequency, for longer than anyone's instruments can date.

At Tier 3, for the first time, the tone seems to change when something significant happens nearby — when an ecosystem is damaged, when a Drowned structure is activated, when a large organism dies. Not dramatically. A half-step shift that lasts for approximately one turn and then returns to baseline. It is the first indication that the tone is responsive rather than passive.

*Writing signal: the tone becomes physical at Tier 3. Write it as something felt before it is heard. The responsiveness should be noted by instruments first; players who feel it without instruments have noticed something important.*

### Body

Cold and pressured. Thermal protection is essential; the equipment is working continuously. Pressure at Tier 3 is the first that requires mechanical assistance — without the right rating, the body begins to report problems. Nothing immediately dangerous at the top of the tier, but present and accumulating.

The current here is the first place the breath-current can be felt.

The breath-current is not a tidal or thermal phenomenon. It is too slow, too regular, and its source is not identifiable by any instrument. It moves against the local hydrological flow — not violently, a gentle consistent pressure that comes from below and slightly to one side, as if the water is being exhaled by something very large and very patient. It is strongest in the Lantern Drop's fissures and in the still basins of the Echo Basins. It is absent in the Drift Graveyard.

Players who notice it tend to describe it the same way, without coordination: it feels like being close to something that is breathing.

*Writing signal: introduce the breath-current at Tier 3 as a physical detail, not a dramatic revelation. The players who notice it may not report it. The players who report it may not be believed. Both responses are correct.*

### Biology

Stranger. The organisms at Tier 3 are not analogues for anything; the framework of almost-familiar is gone. Filter feeders the size of a small vehicle, anchored to fissures in the shelf wall, trailing structures that look like antennae and may be. Predators that navigate by the bioluminescence of their prey rather than by sight — their own light suppressed, moving through the patterns they are hunting.

The Void Drifters are the organism that most directly expresses the Tier 3 uncanny. They are neutrally buoyant, very slow, and appear to have no active propulsion. They move with the breath-current. Whether they are following it or producing it is not determinable from observation. They are beautiful and uncomfortable to look at for extended periods, in a way that players should feel but not be able to explain.

*Writing signal: Tier 3 biology should read as purposeful in ways the organisms cannot intend. Avoid describing organisms as intelligent or aware — describe them as behaving as if the environment itself were guiding them.*

### The ruins — Tier 3

The Drowned structures at Tier 3 are intact and deliberately placed. Not grown over — maintained, in the sense that the biological substrate they are built from continues to be alive and active. The structures are tending themselves.

The inscriptions begin here. Not the dense records of Tier 4 — single glyphs, repeated at thresholds, at doorways, at the edges of what seem to be designated spaces. They appear to be warnings, or markers, or both. Players cannot read them. The feeling they produce — careful, specific, the impression that someone very deliberate put them there — is more useful than any translation.

The structures at Tier 3 are larger than at Tier 2, and their geometry is more obviously wrong. The repeating angles from Tier 2 are here at a scale that makes them impossible to rationalise as accident. A chamber 40 metres across with a ceiling that meets the floor at exactly the same angle on all sides. A corridor that slopes downward at a gradient too precise to be natural, leading to a door that is sealed in a way that has not degraded in however long it has been closed.

*Writing signal: Tier 3 ruins feel inhabited even though they are empty. The maintenance-without-maintainer detail is important. Write them as places that are still in use, by something.*

## Tier 4 — The Blackwater Depths

### The experience

Tier 4 is the dark. Not metaphorically — the solar spectrum is completely absent, and the bioluminescence that lit Tier 3 is sparser here, distributed differently, produced by organisms whose light-use is less communicative and more defensive. The dominant visual experience is objects emerging from blackness and returning to it.

This is also where the Drowned become legible as people. The emotional register of Tier 4 should feel like the sensory register: things emerging from the dark that are recognisable, at close range, as having once been alive and arguing and afraid.

### Light

Bioluminescence at Tier 4 is cold and blue-white, high contrast against total darkness. Individual organisms produce it in bursts — a brief illumination, then dark again. The effect is stroboscopic when fauna are active, static when they are not.

The ruins glow. This is the first significant distinction from Tier 3: Drowned structures at Tier 4 produce their own light, from the biological substrate they are built from, and that light is a different frequency from any organism at this depth. It is the wrong blue — slightly too green, slightly too regular, pulsing on the tone's frequency rather than on any biological rhythm. Players who have been tracking the tone will notice this. The ruins are responsive to it.

At the record rooms specifically: the inscribed walls produce faint light along the inscription channels. The density of inscription means some walls glow nearly continuously. Walking through a record room at Tier 4 is walking through a space lit by what the people who built it wrote on the walls.

*Writing signal: Tier 4 light is cold, high contrast, and comes from the wrong places. The ruins' glow should feel like a distinction before it is understood as one.*

### Sound

The tone is the dominant acoustic feature at Tier 4. The biological noise has nearly gone — the fauna here are large, slow, and widely spaced; their acoustic signatures are low-frequency and intermittent. What remains between those signatures is the tone.

At Tier 4, the tone is loud enough to hear without instruments in a quiet moment, though most players will hear it as a low pressure in the ears rather than a sound. Sensitive instruments can now resolve it into a complex signal — not a single frequency but a chord, multiple harmonics in a relationship that is stable and very old. It does not change in response to events here the way it did at Tier 3. At Tier 4, it is continuous and unvarying, which is its own kind of strangeness.

The record rooms have their own acoustic property: the chamber geometries seem designed to amplify the tone. Whether the Drowned built them this way deliberately, or whether the rooms have settled into resonance with it over time, is not determinable.

*Writing signal: the tone is now a presence. At Tier 4 it should be mentioned before the visual description in any read-aloud text. It arrives first.*

### Body

Cold. Tier 4 cold is not the sharp cold of Tier 3 — it is deep and consistent, a cold that the equipment manages but cannot fully offset. Prolonged stays at Tier 4 produce metabolic cost even with full thermal protection; expeditions are time-limited by biology as much as by oxygen.

The pressure at Tier 4 is significant. Without correct equipment it is immediately dangerous. With correct equipment it is a continuous weight — not painful, but present in every movement, every breath. Players should feel it as attention required to manage.

The breath-current is present at Tier 4 but changed. At Tier 3 it was a gentle consistent pressure from below. At Tier 4 it is intermittent — absent for long periods, then present for a turn or two, stronger than before. The pattern of its absence and presence does not match any tidal or thermal schedule. Some expeditions feel it constantly; some feel it not at all. There is no instrument that correlates the variation with any other measurable condition.

*Writing signal: Tier 4 cold and pressure are a background cost. Write them as labour — the effort of being somewhere difficult. The breath-current's intermittency at Tier 4 is more unsettling than its consistency at Tier 3.*

### Biology

Sparse, large, strange. The organisms at Tier 4 are apex-level — nothing here is prey for anything shallower. Their bioluminescence is defensive or predatory rather than communicative; they do not flock or school. They move through the dark in ones and twos.

The Bone Fields' eponymous feature: the skeletal remains of organisms that have no living analogue at any shallower depth. Whatever produced these remains is either extinct or present only at Tier 5. The bones themselves are a biological material that has partially mineralised over geological time — they are as much stone as bone, and they are vast. Players walking through the Bone Fields are walking through the ribs of something that makes the Depth Stalker look small.

The ecology here is at the edge of what can be sustained without extraordinary inputs. The Drowned maintained it through their systems. Without that maintenance, Tier 4 ecosystems are in slow decline — present, functional, but thinning. Players who understand what they are looking at will recognise this as a consequence of the Drowned's disappearance, not a natural state.

*Writing signal: Tier 4 biology should feel like aftermath. The scale of the bones establishes what was here. The current sparseness establishes what is gone.*

## Tier 5 — The Abyssal Core

*Two locations. Two registers. The world's two oldest things.*

---

### The Reactor Site — Fire

*The location toward which Restorationists move. The Sinkhole Gate leads here.*

This is not a natural thermal site. The reactor's descent and the Drowned's deep infrastructure have interacted in ways that Engineering cannot fully model. The result is a site that is geothermally active in patterns that do not match the local geology — irregular, intense, producing heat plumes that make the water visible as distortion.

**Light:** orange and red. The only warm light in the deep ocean, and it is the light of something broken. Bioluminescence here is suppressed or absent — the organisms that survived the reactor's arrival are adapted to unusual radiation and chemistry, not to low light. Some of them glow in frequencies that are not bioluminescence in any biological sense; instruments flag these as reactor fluorescence.

The ruins near the reactor site are damaged — not by the crash directly, but by the thermal and chemical changes that followed it. The biological substrate is partially dead. Walls that glowed at other Tier 4 and 5 sites are dark here. The tone is present but distorted — the reactor's own emissions create interference that breaks the chord into fragments.

**Body:** hot. The first time in the ocean that the threat is heat rather than cold. Thermal protection works in reverse here; the equipment adapted for Tier 4 cold is badly suited to Tier 5 heat, and players who have not prepared will feel it immediately.

The reactor site is the one place in the deep ocean where the Depth seems absent. The breath-current does not reach here. The tone is broken. Whatever the Depth is, it has withdrawn from this specific location — not abandoned it, but unable to be fully present within the reactor's disruption field.

*Writing signal: the reactor site should feel like an intrusion. Everything that makes the deep ocean feel like it has its own logic and presence is muted or absent here. It is the humans' mess, in the Depth's world.*

---

### The Cage Site — Ice

*The location toward which Transcendence and Preservationist players move. The Cold Eye.*

The coldest place in the ocean. The cage's operation produces a thermal sink — drawing heat from the surrounding water as a component of its regulatory function. The Cold Eye is named for what instruments show when they are pointed at its centre: a temperature reading that should not be possible at this depth, in this ocean, on any world where thermodynamics applies normally.

**Light:** none. The Cold Eye produces no light. The organisms here — few, ancient, extremely slow — produce no bioluminescence. The ruins produce no glow. There is total darkness at the Cold Eye, and the darkness is deliberate.

Players who arrive here without significant light sources see nothing. Players who arrive with light sources see the cage.

The cage does not look like engineering. It does not look biological. It looks like both of those things simultaneously, and like neither — a structure that has been here long enough to become part of the geology, that uses the pressure and temperature gradients of the deep ocean as load-bearing elements, that is alive in the way a very old tree is alive — mostly mineral, still growing at the edges, the life in it more structure than process.

It is very large. Larger than the ship. Larger than anything the players have built.

**Sound:** the tone is loudest here. At the Cold Eye, it is not a tone any longer — it is the full chord, resolved, present as something the body receives rather than the ears hear. Players who have the Alien Attunement icon experience it differently from those who do not. For those who do: it is the closest thing to communication the Depth attempts in the game. Not language. Not instruction. Orientation — the feeling of being shown where you are in relation to everything else.

**Body:** cold beyond cold. The equipment manages it. The body manages it. But prolonged presence at the Cold Eye produces something that is not physiological — a stillness, a slowing of thought, an attention that is not quite voluntary. Players sometimes describe Tier 5 expeditions to the Cold Eye as feeling longer than the clock says they were.

The breath-current: constant here. Stronger than anywhere above. At the Cold Eye, it is not ambiguous — it is clearly a respiration, clearly regular, clearly coming from something that is very large and very present and has been here for a very long time.

*Writing signal: the Cold Eye should feel like arrival. Everything in the sensory vocabulary that has been building since Tier 3 — the tone, the breath-current, the Depth's presence — reaches its fullest expression here. Not a revelation, but a recognition. Players who have been paying attention will feel that they have been moving toward this place since the first time they noticed something they couldn't explain.*

## Quick Reference — Sensory Signatures by Tier

| Tier | Light | Sound | Body | Biology | Depth presence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Warm amber-gold, shifting columns, generous | Busy, layered, tone inaudible | Warm, gentle current, manageable | Abundant, almost-familiar, quiet alienness | Absent |
| 2 | Blue-green, directional, shaft-light through kelp | Structured, thinner; tone audible as data | Cold thermocline, complex current | Specialised, purposeful-looking kelp | Absent |
| 3 | Biological, patterned, no solar source | Spare; tone physical, responsive to events | Cold, pressured; breath-current first appears | Stranger, behaviorally guided-seeming | First traces — tone responsive, bioluminescence patterned |
| 4 | Cold blue-white strobes; ruins glow wrong | Tone dominant, nearly continuous, a chord | Deep cold, heavy pressure; breath-current intermittent | Sparse, apex-scale, ecology in decline | Consistent — tone audible, ruins resonant |
| 5 (fire) | Orange-red, reactor fluorescence, warm light | Tone broken by reactor interference | Hot, reversed thermal threat | Radiation-adapted, suppressed | Withdrawn — reactor disrupts Depth presence |
| 5 (ice) | None — total darkness | Full chord, bodily reception | Deepest cold; breath-current constant, strong | Ancient, minimal, extremely slow | Complete — orientation, breath-current, direct attunement possible |

## Phrases to use / phrases to avoid

### Use
- *the tone* (not "the signal", not "the frequency" in player-facing text — instruments use those words, people use *the tone*)
- *the breath-current* (always hyphenated; always described as something felt before it is measured)
- *wrong blue* (for the ruins' glow at Tier 4)
- *almost-familiar* (for Tier 1–2 biology — always hyphenated)
- *the chord* (for the tone at Tier 4–5, when it resolves from a single frequency)
- *patterned* (for bioluminescence at Tier 3 — not *coordinated*, not *deliberate*, not *intelligent*)
- *tending* (for the Depth's activity — the ocean *tends*, it does not *acts* or *decides*)

### Avoid
- *alien* as a standalone adjective — it stops description rather than advancing it
- *eerie*, *uncanny*, *haunting* — the sensory vocabulary should produce these feelings without naming them
- *intelligent* applied to the Depth or any ecosystem — even at Tier 5, the Depth does not think; it *tends*
- *breathing* as a direct metaphor at Tier 3 — save it for Tier 5; at Tier 3 it should only approach that word
- *warm* at Tiers 3 and 4 — cold is the register; warmth is the reactor's specific signature at Tier 5
- descriptions that require the player to have read the lore — every sensory detail should land for someone who hasn't read the Drowned brief
