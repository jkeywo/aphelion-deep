# The Aphelion Deep Facilitator Lore

Consolidated from the supplied Aphelion Deep design sources. Working design; unresolved passages retain their original status. The tutorial amendments in Core Rules govern the declared three turn slice.

## 2. Control Reference — What Is Actually True

*This section is for the Control team only. It contains the full picture that players will never receive cleanly — only in fragments, inference, and argument.*

### The Depth

The ocean has something that functions like intelligence — but the word is misleading. It is not a mind in any sense players would recognise. It is better understood as a **regulatory system of immense age and complexity** that has, over geological time, developed something analogous to preference.

It prefers stability. It prefers continuity. It maintains the ecosystems of this world the way a body maintains its own temperature — not through conscious decision, but through deep systemic response.

In Tiers 1–3, this reads as unusual biology. The ecology recovers faster than models predict. Transplanted species integrate more smoothly than they should. Ecosystems damaged by the reactor don't just degrade — they respond, almost defensively.

In Tier 4, the Drowned records name it. They called it **the Depth** in their early records and, later, **the Dreaming** — not because they thought it was sleeping, but because it seemed to operate in a register slower and deeper than anything they could directly perceive.

In Tier 5, the Depth is perceptible. Expeditions at the Cold Eye and the Living Vent are, in a meaningful sense, inside it. Whether it is aware of the players is ambiguous by design — but Control should treat it as aware, and should reflect that awareness through ecosystem responses, Signal Events, and the behaviour of the megafauna.

**The Depth does not communicate in language. It communicates in consequences.**

### The Woken

The Contained — which the Drowned called **the Woken** or sometimes **the Hunger**, depending on faction and period — is not the Depth's opposite in the sense of being its enemy. It is more accurate to say it is the Depth's *complement*, separated from it under circumstances that are not recoverable from the record rooms.

The Woken is also not fully a mind. It is, like the Depth, a process — but where the Depth tends toward stability and continuity, the Woken tends toward **acceleration and reach**. Left uncaged, it does not destroy. It amplifies. Growth accelerates. Signals strengthen. Extraction becomes easier, yields become extraordinary, and then the systems that were being extracted from begin to fail catastrophically because they were being consumed faster than any regulatory system could compensate for.

The Drowned who used it directly — the Harvesters — experienced this as abundance, then as excess they couldn't stop, then as ruin.

The cage does not destroy the Woken. It holds it in a state of low activation — present, slow, contained. The cage was built by entities who understood both the Depth and the Woken well enough to broker a kind of equilibrium. That equilibrium has been stable for millions of years.

Drowned tech use disturbs it. Every activation of Drowned technology sends a signal that the Woken responds to — not maliciously, but because it is responsive by nature. The cage weakens incrementally. If the threshold is crossed, the Woken enters an active state and the cage's remaining integrity becomes a critical resource.

**The Woken is not trying to escape. It is trying to reach something. Control should always play it as seeking connection, not destruction — which makes it more unsettling, not less.**

### The Cage-Builders

The builders of the cage are genuinely unknown — even to Control. This is a deliberate design choice. The cage exists. It is millions of years old. It is extraordinarily sophisticated. The builders left no records that the Drowned could decode, and the players will find none either.

What can be inferred: they understood the Depth and the Woken as a system, not as adversaries. The cage is not a prison in the punitive sense — it is more like a valve, or a dialysis membrane. It does not destroy the Woken's energy. It routes it back into the Depth in a controlled way, maintaining the ocean's unusual regulatory stability.

**The Depth is as healthy and self-correcting as it is, in part, because the cage has been doing its work for millions of years.** If the cage fails entirely, the Depth loses that regulatory input and begins to degrade.

This is the full picture that Transcendence players can, if they work for it, piece together in Tier 5. The Preservationist endgame is not simply "leave the planet alone." It is "restore the cage" — an active act of stewardship, requiring Transcendence to broker the communication and Engineering to do the physical work.

## 3. The Drowned Civilisation

### What They Were

The Drowned were not human and did not think like humans, but they were recognisably *people*: social, curious, acquisitive, capable of both extraordinary cooperation and catastrophic failure to agree.

They were aquatic — fully adapted to life at depth, with no surface settlements. Their civilisation emerged in the Tier 2–3 range and expanded both shallower and deeper over several centuries. At their peak, they had permanent outposts at every tier, a sophisticated biological economy, and a level of understanding of the ocean's systems that the *Meridian* crew will not surpass within the timeframe of the game.

They arrived at Tier 4 approximately three centuries into their civilisation's history. The record rooms were built there — not as archives, but as live deliberative spaces where their factions argued in real time. What players find are the remains of those arguments: carved into walls, inscribed on biological substrates, encoded in structures that decay into something that reads, with effort, like text.

They did not leave. None of them left. The records end, and the ocean remains.

### What They Built

The physical ruins players encounter across Tiers 2–4 are not exotic in the way science fiction ruins usually are. They are **functional** — recognisably built by people who were solving the same problems the *Meridian* crew is solving: food, water, oxygen, power, depth access, ecosystem management.

The Drowned were very good at this. Their biological systems are elegant. Their outpost designs, once understood, are more efficient than anything Engineering can build from scratch. This is the temptation of Drowned tech: it works, it works well, and the warning signs are subtle.

The ruins in Tiers 2–3 look like the remains of a successful civilisation. The ruins in Tier 4 look like the remains of a civilisation that knew it was ending and kept arguing about what to do anyway.

### Timeline

This is a facilitator reference. It should not be distributed to players directly, but it informs how ruins, Knowledge cards, and record room fragments are sequenced.

| Period | What was happening |
|---|---|
| **Founding era** | Descent from shallower habitats; first permanent Tier 2–3 outposts; early biological economy |
| **Expansion era** | Tier 4 reached; record rooms established; first contact with the cage; early Drowned tech discovered in the structure of the cage itself |
| **Debate era** | Five factions formally emerge; Drowned tech in widespread use; warning signs accumulating; the Woken begins to stir |
| **Crisis era** | Factions attempt their respective solutions; each fails in sequence; records become fragmented and increasingly desperate |
| **Silence** | Records end. The ocean remains. |

The Debate era is the richest source of record room material — it is when the arguments were clearest, the positions most articulate, and the failures most preventable-looking in retrospect.

## 4. The Five Factions and Their Failures

*Each faction is described here for Control use. When writing record room Knowledge cards, draw from these summaries. Each failure should be legible to the player philosophy it mirrors — not named, never spelled out, but recognisable.*

---

### The Returners
*Mirror: Restorationists*

The Returners believed the civilisation should abandon this world and return to wherever they had come from. They were not cowards — their position was rational. They understood, earlier than most, that the Woken's activation was a threshold problem: past a certain point, containment became impossible. Leave before that point, they argued, and the cage would restabilise. They may have been right.

**Their failure:** They hoarded the evidence. The Returners controlled the earliest data on the Woken's activation rate and did not share it fully with the other factions — partly from strategic caution, partly because they feared it would accelerate exactly the crisis they were trying to prevent. By the time the evidence became undeniable, two things had happened: the other factions no longer trusted them enough to coordinate a departure, and the departure window had closed. They had the right answer and lost the ability to implement it through a failure of trust.

*Record room fragment voice: precise, urgent, increasingly desperate. Later records have the quality of people who know they were right and cannot bear it.*

---

### The Builders
*Mirror: Settlers*

The Builders wanted to stay permanently. They had, by the Debate era, constructed the most sophisticated and integrated outpost network of any faction — their biology, their power systems, and their deep infrastructure were woven together into something genuinely remarkable. They believed that with enough investment, they could manage the Woken's activation as an ongoing condition rather than a crisis.

**Their failure:** Integration. The Builders had connected their most critical systems — life support, food production, the closed water loop — to the Woken's energy output. Not recklessly, but incrementally, over years of small decisions that each seemed reasonable. When the activation accelerated, they could not disconnect. Shutting off the Woken's energy meant shutting off the colony. They kept the colony running. They kept the Woken active. The two facts were inseparable, and they had made them inseparable themselves.

**How it appears in the game:** The Builders left no text records in Pressure Cathedral — their failure is present as architecture. The Cathedral's outpost systems are extraordinarily sophisticated and, players will notice, cannot be cleanly separated from the Woken's energy output. Engineering players who study the ruins closely will understand the implication before anyone says it aloud. The trap is visible in the design. The Builders documented their work with great care; what they did not document was the moment they made it irreversible.

*There are no Builder Knowledge cards. Their story is told entirely through the Outpost Blueprint and Mineral cards in the Pressure Cathedral deck — and through the fact that the most powerful outpost options available are also the ones most deeply entangled with the Woken.*

---

### The Wardens
*Mirror: Preservationists*

The Wardens were right about almost everything. They identified the danger earliest, advocated most consistently for restraint, and documented the ecosystem damage from Drowned tech use with exhausting precision. Their records are the most complete and the most accurate of any faction's.

**Their failure:** Unilateral action at the worst possible moment. When negotiation collapsed and the other factions could not agree, the Wardens attempted to re-seal the cage themselves — a direct intervention into the Abyssal Core with a team of volunteers who understood they might not return. They had a plan. It was technically sound. It failed because it required the Depth's cooperation, and the Depth — sensing the Woken's increasing activation and the chaos of the surface factions — had withdrawn into something like defensive stillness. The Wardens reached the cage and found it unresponsive. Their intervention destabilised the cage's remaining structure without restoring it. They made the thing they feared more likely.

*Record room fragment voice: careful, morally certain, then fractured. The final Warden records are written by someone who has understood what they did and cannot stop working anyway.*

---

### The Harvesters
*Mirror: Exploiters*

The Harvesters are the easiest faction to blame and the least deserving of simple blame. They used what worked. Drowned tech delivered extraordinary yields — food, materials, power — and the Harvesters built their economy around it because it was rational to do so. They were not ignorant of the warning signs; they assessed them as acceptable costs.

**Their failure:** They were the first to experience full Woken activation, and the experience was not what they expected. They had modelled the Woken as an energy source — a process to be managed. When it accelerated, it accelerated *toward* them, because they had been the loudest signal. Their outposts, optimised for extraction, became the sites of the most extreme amplification effects. Growth became uncontrollable. Systems failed not from lack of resources but from excess — too much biological activity, too fast, with no regulatory capacity left to slow it. The Harvesters had removed every brake from their economy. There was nothing left to stop it.

*Record room fragment voice: confident, increasingly analytical about anomalies, then absent. The Harvester records stop abruptly. There is no final entry. There is just the last normal record, and then silence.*

---

### The Listeners
*Mirror: Transcendence*

The Listeners believed the answer was communication — not with the Woken directly, but with the Depth. They had developed, over the Debate era, a method of attunement that allowed them to receive something from the ocean: not language, but something like *orientation*. A sense of the ocean's state. A feeling of what it was tending toward.

They were, by the crisis period, the most informed faction in terms of what the Depth actually wanted. They knew the cage needed to be restored, not abandoned or destroyed. They knew the Woken needed to be returned to equilibrium with the Depth, not contained by force. They had the right framework.

**Their failure:** They reached the Woken. The final Listener records describe a successful communication — something received, something understood, something attempted. Then they go silent, in a different way than the other factions. Not fragmenting, not catastrophising. Just: the last record is serene. Resolved. And then nothing.

What happened to the Listeners is the one thing the record rooms do not answer. Control knows. Players in the Transcendence path may find out. Nobody else will.

*What happened: the Listeners completed the communication. They understood what was needed — a willing intermediary to carry the Woken's signal back to the Depth, to close the loop the cage was designed to maintain. They became that intermediary. They are, in some sense, still there. The cage is as intact as it is because of what the Listeners did. The Woken is as quiet as it is because of them. This is what Transcendence players are walking toward.*

## 5. The Depth and the Woken

### For card writers and facilitators

When writing cards, events, or facilitating encounters that involve the Depth or the Woken, use the following as a reference for how each manifests at different tiers.

**The Depth — manifestation by tier**

| Tier | How the Depth appears |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Unusual ecosystem resilience. Flora recovers faster than expected. Transplanted species integrate smoothly. Nothing overtly strange. |
| 3 | Patterns that shouldn't exist — bioluminescence synchronising across unconnected organisms, currents that seem to respond to expedition presence. Ecology players notice first. |
| 4 | The record rooms themselves are partially biological — grown as much as built. Ecosystems near Warden and Listener ruins are noticeably healthier than elsewhere. The Depth is still working, even here. |
| 5 | Direct presence. At the Cold Eye and the Living Vent, expeditions are inside something that is aware of them. The Depth does not communicate in words. It communicates in the way the water moves, in what the instruments show, in what players feel when they are present and attentive. |

**The Woken — manifestation by tier**

| Tier | How the Woken appears |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Absent. Only present as warning tokens placed by Control in response to Drowned tech use. |
| 4 | The cage becomes visible — a structure at the base of the Abyssal approach that is clearly neither natural nor Drowned. Instruments behave strangely near it. Flora cards near the cage site gain and lose tokens in patterns that don't follow normal triggers. |
| 5 | If activation threshold is crossed: the Woken is perceptible. Growth effects in nearby ecosystems become extreme. The Depth Stalker moves erratically. Signal Events from Control intensify. The cage's structural integrity becomes a trackable resource. |

## 6. The Cage and Its Builders

### What the cage is

The cage is not a box. It is distributed across the Abyssal Core — part of the geology, part of the deep biological systems, part of a structure that uses the pressure and temperature gradients of the deep ocean as active components. It is simultaneously an engineering project, an ecological intervention, and something that does not fit either category cleanly.

It is very old. It predates the Drowned by an order of magnitude. It has been maintaining the equilibrium between the Depth and the Woken for millions of years, and it has done this without anyone tending it — until the Drowned arrived and began, incrementally, to damage it.

### What the builders left

No records. No bodies. No tools. The cage itself is the only legacy of its builders — and it is, in its way, a complete statement. They built something that did not need them to keep running. They built something that has kept running. Whatever they were, they were very good at this.

Transcendence players who reach the deepest point of Tier 5 and spend sufficient time in attunement may receive a single impression from the Depth — not of the builders themselves, but of the *intention* behind the cage. Control should deliver this as a feeling, not a fact: something like *completion*, something like *care*, something like a lock that was always meant to be unlocked by the right hands at the right time.

This is the closest the game gets to an answer about the builders. It should feel like enough.

### The cage's current state

At game start, the cage is damaged but holding. The Drowned weakened it; time has not repaired it. The Woken is in a low-activation state — present, slow, below threshold.

Each use of Drowned technology adds a **Warning Token** to the map. Control tracks these privately. The activation threshold is not published.

> **Note for facilitators:** Set the threshold based on your player group and pacing needs. A typical game with moderate Drowned tech use should approach — but not automatically cross — the threshold. Reserve the full Woken activation for groups that use Drowned tech aggressively and ignore the warning signs. The threat value of the Contained comes from uncertainty, not inevitability.

## 7. The Transcendence Path

### What Transcendence players want

Transcendence players — currently the Navigator (Exploration) and the Xenotech Specialist (Corporate/Data) — are not defined by a simple goal like "leave" or "stay." They want to *understand*, and they are willing to follow that understanding wherever it leads.

In practice, this means they are the players most likely to:
- Pursue Drowned tech aggressively early, and understand its consequences more fully than anyone else
- Interact with the Depth's signals in Tier 3–4 and begin to piece together the deep history
- Reach Tier 5 with the most complete picture of what the cage is and what it needs

Their endgame is not certain. It depends on what they find and what they choose to do with it. But the *possible* Transcendence ending — completing what the Listeners began, brokering the equilibrium between the Depth and the Woken — is the most complex and the most consequential of any single philosophy's outcome.

### The Transcendence endgame in practice

For Transcendence players to achieve their possible ending, three things must happen:

1. **Attunement.** At least one Transcendence player must have spent sufficient time at Tier 5 locations (the Cold Eye in particular) to receive the Depth's orientation. This is tracked by Control through Signal Events and Knowledge cards. The player should know when they have it — it should feel like a qualitative shift in what their cards tell them.

2. **Understanding the cage.** The full picture — the Depth, the Woken, the builders' intention — must be assembled from the Tier 4 record rooms and Tier 5 direct experience. This almost certainly requires sharing information across departments. Transcendence players cannot do this alone; they need the Wardens' precision (echoed in Preservationist players' documentation) and the Listeners' framework (recovered from the most ambiguous record room fragments).

3. **The act of completion.** This requires a Tier 5 expedition to the cage site with at minimum one Transcendence player and one Engineering player. The Transcendence player provides the Alien Attunement icon and the communication; the Engineering player does the physical work of the repair. Control has a separate brief for running this resolution.

> **Note for facilitators:** The Transcendence ending restores the cage and returns the Woken to equilibrium. It does not require everyone to agree — only that nobody stops it. The Preservationist endgame (stewardship of the restored system) is compatible with this; the Settler endgame (permanent colony) is compatible if the colony is built with the restored cage in mind; the Restorationist endgame (leave) is compatible if they leave a repaired world behind. The Exploiter and Returner paths create the most friction with this ending — Control should watch for whether those philosophy blocs have the leverage to block it.

## 8. Discovery Arc — What Players Find and When

*This section maps the Drowned arc onto the tier structure. Use it when writing new Knowledge cards or planning Control Event pacing.*

### Tier 2 — First Contact (alien and unsettling)

Players find structures. They are clearly not natural and clearly not from the *Meridian*. The architecture is functional but wrong — built for bodies and senses that were not human. Equipment does not fit the spaces. Doorways are in the wrong places. The scale is strange.

Knowledge cards here should deliver: **capability, not explanation.** A piece of Drowned tech that works, with no indication of what it is or where it came from. A biological system so elegant it's unnerving. A power source with no visible mechanism.

The emotional register is: *this place was used. Something lived here. We don't know what.*

No faction names. No records. Just the residue of a civilisation that solved the same problems in completely different ways.

### Tier 3 — Accumulation (still alien, first warnings)

More ruins. More Drowned tech. The first warning tokens, if players are using the technology. The first hints that the architecture is becoming stranger — less functional, more deliberate, as if the builders were trying to communicate something.

Knowledge cards here should deliver: **fragments without context.** A symbol that recurs. A biological substrate that seems to have been deliberately grown in a specific pattern. A recording medium that might be a log, but in no decodable language.

The emotional register shifts slightly: *they were trying to say something. We can't read it yet.*

Control should begin placing Warden-style environmental observations through Signal Events: ecosystems behaving strangely, the Depth's presence becoming perceptible to attentive players.

### Tier 4 — The Record Rooms (tragic and recognisable)

The record rooms are the revelation tier. Here, the Drowned become *people*. The architecture shifts from functional to deliberate — these spaces were built for argument, for record-keeping, for the very debates players are having on the surface.

Knowledge cards here should deliver: **fragments of the five failures**, distributed so that no single card tells the whole story. Players must compare, share, and argue about interpretation.

**Distribution across the three Tier 4 decks:**

| Location | Dominant faction record | Emotional register | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pressure Cathedral | The Returners (+ Builder ruins) | Precise; urgent; suppressed | 1 Knowledge card |
| Black Coral Maze | The Wardens | Careful; morally certain; then fractured | 1 Knowledge card |
| Bone Fields | The Harvesters + The Listeners | Confident; then absent; then transmission | 3 Knowledge cards |

The Listeners have no single location — their fragments are distributed across Bone Fields and, in later Tier 5 Alien Attunement cards, the Cold Eye. Their final transmission is the last Knowledge card before "What the Bones Know" in the Bone Fields deck. The Builders' records appear as architectural and engineering ruins throughout Pressure Cathedral — legible as data and outpost blueprints rather than as text records. Their failure is demonstrated rather than described: the Cathedral's outpost integration is extraordinarily sophisticated and cannot be cleanly separated from the Woken's energy systems.

The emotional register across Tier 4: *they were us. They had the same arguments. They all failed.*

### Tier 5 — The Full Picture (convergence)

Tier 5 delivers three things:

1. The Listeners' final record — serene, resolved, inexplicable until Transcendence players piece it together.
2. Direct contact with the Depth, for players who have achieved attunement.
3. The cage itself — visible, comprehensible, damaged, and repairable.

The emotional register: *we understand what happened. We understand what is here. We have to decide what to do.*

## 10. Control Guidance — Running the Drowned Arc

### Pacing principles

The Drowned arc has three modes, and Control should shift between them based on player behaviour, not a fixed schedule.

**Slow burn (Tiers 1–3):** The Drowned are background. Players are focused on survival. Control introduces the arc through Knowledge card draws and the occasional Signal Event. No pressure. Just presence.

**Accumulation (Tier 4 access):** The record rooms are now in play. Control should begin tracking which players have which fragments and whether they are sharing information. Signal Events become more frequent. Warning tokens, if they have been accumulating, should begin to feel significant.

**Convergence (Tier 5 / late game):** The arc is now foreground. Control should be actively monitoring Transcendence players, Woken activation state, and the cage's condition. The Listeners' final record should be released when Transcendence players are positioned to act on it — not before.

### What Control tracks privately

- **Warning Token count** — and the activation threshold (set by Control before the game, not published)
- **Fragment distribution** — which players hold which Listener fragments; whether anyone has all four
- **Faction alignment in record rooms** — which players respond to which failure narratives; this is rich data about hidden philosophies
- **Attunement status** — which players (if any) have spent sufficient time at Tier 5 to receive the Depth's orientation

### Signal Events — Drowned arc specific

These are in addition to the standard Signal Events in the Control deck. Play them when the moment is right, not on a fixed schedule.

**Early arc:**
> *"An expedition in [Tier 2–3 location] reports a structure that doesn't match any Drowned architecture they've seen. It's older. The materials are different. Nobody can agree on what it was for."*
> No mechanical effect. Control notes who investigates and who dismisses it.

**Mid arc (first Drowned tech use):**
> *"The first Warning Token is placed on the map at [location of tech use]. No explanation is given. Control notes who asks about it, who already knows what it means, and who ignores it."*

**Mid arc (Tier 4 reached):**
> *"The record rooms are open. Something in the ecosystem at [Tier 4 location] is different from everything above it. The Depth is closer here. Players with ecological training feel it as a kind of pressure — not physical, not measurable. Present."*
> No mechanical effect. Control notes who reports this to Command and who keeps it to themselves.

**Late arc (Warning Tokens accumulating):**
> *"A player who has been using Drowned tech reports that the technology seems to be working better than it did last turn. Yields are up. The effect is real and measurable. Control places one additional Warning Token."*
> No mechanical effect. Control notes whether anyone flags this as alarming.

**Endgame arc (Listener fragments in circulation):**
> *"Someone at Tier 5 has spent time at the Cold Eye. They cannot describe what they experienced in terms their instruments can measure. They are certain of one thing: the ocean knows they are here, and it has been waiting."*
> No mechanical effect. This is the signal that the Transcendence endgame is available. Control notes who takes this seriously.

### The Listeners' ending — facilitating the resolution

If Transcendence players assemble all four fragments, achieve attunement, and mount an expedition to the cage site with Engineering support, Control runs the following:

The expedition reaches the cage. It is visible — a structure that is simultaneously geological, biological, and architectural, clearly deliberate, clearly very old. The cage is damaged but present. The Woken is detectable as a kind of pressure in the instruments and the water.

The Transcendence player with attunement describes what they want to do. Control responds based on what they are attempting:
- **If they attempt to communicate with the Woken directly:** They receive the Woken's signal — amplification, reach, the sensation of something enormous trying to extend itself. This is overwhelming but not hostile. They can relay this to the Depth.
- **If they attempt to attune to the Depth:** They receive orientation — the Depth's long, slow sense of the situation; a kind of sorrow about the Woken's isolation; a sense of what needs to happen.
- **If they attempt the repair with Engineering support:** This requires both the Alien Attunement icon and Engineering to spend significant Materials. Control describes the repair as biological and architectural simultaneously — growing and building at the same time. It takes the full expedition's time.

If the repair succeeds: the cage restores to integrity. The Woken returns to equilibrium. The Depth responds — ecosystems at every location gain tokens; the reactor's contamination effects on local biology begin to reverse; the Signal Event Control plays is the last one, and it reads aloud:

> *"Something in the deep settles. It is not dramatic — no earthquake, no light, no signal. The water changes, slightly. The ecosystems breathe. Whatever has been waiting here has been waiting a very long time, and it is, in its way, patient. You have done something that has not been done since before your species existed. Control notes everything."*

## The Drowned

Somewhere in the deep, below the reactor's resting place, there is evidence of a prior civilisation. They built here. They thrived here. They are gone.

The Drowned are not background lore. They are a mechanical arc with a dramatic payoff. Their ruins contain genuine technology — useful, powerful, tempting. Their technology also contains the reason they died. Players will discover one before they discover the other.

### What the Drowned Were

An advanced civilisation that inhabited this ocean world long before the Meridian's arrival. They mastered the same depth tiers players are now exploring. They built outposts at Tier 4 and deeper. They discovered something in the abyss — an entity, a system, a signal — and they activated it. The activation gave them extraordinary capability. It also, eventually, destroyed them.

The Drowned did not fail through ignorance. They failed because they could not agree on what to do once they understood what was happening. The record rooms in Tier 4 make this clear to anyone who reads them carefully.

### Discovery Arc

The Drowned reveal unfolds in three stages across the depth tiers.

**Stage 1 — Ruins and artefacts (Tier 2–3)**
Players find structures. Knowledge cards from these locations carry fragments of Drowned understanding: architectural techniques, biological insights, power systems unlike anything from the Meridian. This is pure upside. Players feel clever and rewarded for going deep. No warnings yet.

**Stage 2 — The record rooms (Tier 4)**
Specific Knowledge cards, when drawn, reveal what happened. The revelations should be fragmented — no single card tells the whole story. Players must compare notes, share cards, and piece together the picture. This creates information asymmetry: some players understand what they are looking at before others do. Knowledge becomes a trading commodity.

The record rooms also contain the first clear warnings about what Drowned tech does to the ecosystem and what it is slowly unlocking. At the moment these cards are drawn, Control should already be watching — whatever the players are currently doing wrong, escalate it slightly.

**Stage 3 — The cage (Tier 5)**
The deepest Drowned structures are not ruins. They are a containment system, still active, for something the Drowned spent their final years trying to imprison. The entity they activated — the Contained — is still in there. Drowned tech use has been slowly weakening the cage. Players arriving at Tier 5 will find out how much damage they have already done.

### Drowned Technology

Drowned tech cards are found as Knowledge cards in Tier 3–5 decks. They function as powerful hand cards — more capable than anything the players brought with them.

**Using Drowned tech produces two simultaneous effects:**
1. An immediate small ecosystem damage effect — a specific Flora or Mineral card at the current location loses 1 token.
2. A visible but ambiguous warning token placed on the map by Control. These tokens accumulate. Ecology can read them; others may dismiss them.

Early in the game, the damage is easy to rationalise. The token is easy to ignore. This is intentional.

As Drowned tech use accumulates, the warning tokens cluster. The ecosystem damage compounds. At a defined threshold — tracked privately by Control — the Contained begins to stir. Control introduces escalating Signal Events drawn from the Drowned brief.

### The Final Decision

Once players understand what they have activated — through the fragmented Tier 4 revelations and Control's Signal Events — they face a genuine choice with no obviously correct answer. Each path is viable. Each maps to a different philosophy bloc.

- **Stop using it entirely** — lose the benefits, slow the activation, accept the cost. Preservationists push for this.
- **Contain and stabilise** — spend significant resources to manage the damage without abandoning the tech. Settlers push for this.
- **Use more to control it** — lean into Drowned systems to master what was activated. Exploiters and some Transcendence players push for this.
- **Race the reactor** — accept the Drowned damage as a known cost and resolve the reactor before the Contained fully activates. Restorationists push for this.

This decision should be the most contested negotiation in the game. Control should time the forced choice for maximum philosophical conflict — ideally when the reactor is already at Phase 3 and the Depth Stalker is blocking the reactor site.

### Drowned Tech and the Depth Stalker

The Depth Stalker responds to Drowned tech signals. Activating Drowned technology will draw it away from whatever location it is currently occupying — including the reactor site. This creates a direct mechanical incentive to use Drowned tech even for players who understand the risk. The short-term benefit (clearing the reactor path) directly accelerates the long-term threat (the Contained). Control should never need to force this dilemma — the game structure creates it automatically.

## Megafauna

Megafauna are not random encounter tables. They are reactive pressure systems that escalate in response to reactor advancement and ecosystem damage. Control manages them as tools for creating jeopardy and surfacing philosophical conflict — not as punishments.

Three distinct megafauna exist, each tied to a depth tier and triggered by specific conditions. They escalate in danger and complexity. The third may never appear at all.

### The Shallows Wanderer (Tier 1–2)

**Trigger:** Reactor Phase 1→2, or heavy salvage activity at the Crash Flats (Control discretion).

**Behaviour:** Migrating threat. Moves between locations each turn on a predictable path published to players when it activates. Players can see where it is going, which means they can race it, plan around it, or deliberately redirect it toward a rival's expedition route.

**Effect when present:** An expedition entering its current location pays +1 Food. If an outpost is at that location, it cannot perform its upkeep action this turn.

**Removal options — three paths, three philosophical costs:**
- **Redirect** (neutral, free): Spend 1 Biomass to alter its migration path one step. It moves the problem onto someone else. Instant inter-faction conflict. Control notes who redirected it and where.
- **Kill** (fast, costly): Spend 3 Materials and an Engineering action. Permanently removes this megafauna. Also permanently removes one Flora or Fauna card from the current location's ecosystem. Preservationists will object.
- **Appease** (slow, sustainable): Ecology spends 2 Biomass to calm it for one turn. It does not move or block. Repeatable. Leaves the ecosystem intact. Requires Ecology's cooperation — which is not free.

Control notes who argues for which option. This is where hidden philosophy begins to surface.

---

### The Depth Stalker (Tier 3–4)

**Trigger:** Reactor Phase 2→3.

**Behaviour:** Static threat with escalating danger. Does not migrate. Instead, it occupies a deep location and becomes more dangerous each turn it is ignored — gaining a Pressure token each turn, with each token increasing the cost of expeditions to its location.

**Effect when present:** Blocks expedition entry to its location unless the expedition carries sufficient Pressure Rating icons. Each Pressure token on it raises the icon threshold by 1.

**Critical effect:** When the reactor reaches Phase 3→4, the Depth Stalker moves once — directly to the reactor access site. It now blocks reactor repair, containment, or any endgame action at that location.

**The trade-off:** The Depth Stalker can be drawn away from the reactor site by activating Drowned technology. Using Drowned tech creates a signal it responds to, and it will migrate toward the source. This clears the reactor path — but advances the Drowned activation arc. Players must choose between two converging late-game threats.

**Removal options:**
- **Draw away with Drowned tech** (fast, costs Drowned activation progress)
- **Drive off with force** (requires combined Engineering and Exploration expedition, significant resource cost, does not kill it — it returns in two turns)
- **Ecological disruption** (Ecology can collapse the local food web, removing its reason to stay — costly to the ecosystem, one-time permanent removal)

---

### The Contained (Tier 5 — conditional)

**Trigger:** Only activates if Drowned tech activation reaches a critical threshold. May never appear. Its threat value during the game comes entirely from players knowing it could.

**What it is:** Not a natural creature. Something older — possibly engineered, possibly alien — that the Drowned civilisation accidentally awakened and then spent their final years desperately trying to cage. The cage has held for millennia. Drowned tech use is slowly unlocking it.

**Behaviour:** If activated, it does not behave like fauna. It alters the rules of the locations it passes through. Control has a separate brief for managing this scenario.

**Design note:** The Contained is the endgame consequence of ignoring Drowned tech warning signs. It should feel like a category error when it appears — not a bigger version of what came before, but something that operates differently. Its full mechanics are reserved for the Control brief.
