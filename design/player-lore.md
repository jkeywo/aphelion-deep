## 1. Player-Facing Handout — The Deep History

### What the Ocean Knows

This world is not empty. It was not empty before you arrived, and it was not empty before the civilisation that came before you arrived.

The ocean is approximately four billion years old. Life here is older than life on your homeworld. The ecosystems you are learning to read — the filterweed, the reef formations, the deep-water chemistries — are the surface expression of something that has been working for longer than your species has existed.

In the deepest parts of this ocean, there are two things that predate everything else.

The first is the ocean itself — not merely as water and chemistry, but as something more. The currents here do not behave entirely as fluid dynamics would predict. Ecosystems damaged by exploitation recover faster than they should, as if something is correcting for the loss. At Tier 5, the bioluminescence patterns are not random. Expeditions that reach the Cold Eye report a sensation that their instruments cannot measure: the feeling of being *noticed*.

The second is the cage.

The cage is older than the civilisation that came before you. It is built into the geological structure of the Abyssal Core — part architecture, part biology, part something that has no category in your engineering vocabulary. It has been here, and it has been holding, for longer than anyone can calculate.

Something is inside it.

The civilisation that came before you — the ones whose ruins you have been exploring since Tier 2 — did not build the cage. They found it. They spent several centuries studying it. And then, in five different ways, through five different factions with five different philosophies, they broke it.

They are gone now.

The cage is still holding. Barely. And you have been using the technology they left behind.
