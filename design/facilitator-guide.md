# The Aphelion Deep Facilitator Guidance

Consolidated from the supplied Aphelion Deep design sources. Working design; unresolved passages retain their original status. The tutorial amendments in Core Rules govern the declared three turn slice.

## Important Design Risks

### Outpost Spam
Players may try to maximize outpost count.

Possible controls:
- Upkeep increases with number of outposts
- Staffing requirements
- Diminishing returns on shallow sites
- Resource pressure from expansion

### Idle Time
Expedition-heavy play can leave players waiting.

Possible controls:
- Parallel expedition resolution
- Micro-roles within expeditions
- Department-level upkeep tasks

### Dominant Strategy: Go Deep Immediately
Deep locations may become obviously best if not constrained.

Possible controls:
- Prerequisites for access
- Instability costs
- Staffing demands
- Ecological or technical gating

### Weak Philosophy Impact
If philosophy does not change actual decisions, it becomes cosmetic.

Possible controls:
- Distinct scoring tied to system outcomes
- Philosophy-linked incentives
- Endgame reveal and scoring
- Soft win conditions for more than one philosophy

### Drowned Tech as Obvious Best Option
If Drowned tech is powerful enough, players may use it regardless of warning signs — treating the warning tokens as an acceptable tax.

Possible controls:
- Warning token effects must compound visibly and painfully (not just minor ecosystem damage)
- The Depth Stalker's attraction to Drowned tech signals must feel like an immediate, meaningful consequence — not a future abstraction
- The Contained's activation threshold should not be publicly known; uncertainty is the pressure

### Reactor Slowdown Captured by One Bloc
If a single philosophy bloc controls all reactor-relevant outposts early, they can either hold the reactor hostage or solve it unilaterally, collapsing the negotiation dynamic.

Possible controls:
- Reactor slowdown outposts should require inputs from at least two departments to build and maintain
- No single outpost should be sufficient to slow the reactor alone — combination is always required
- Control should monitor outpost concentration and inject events that challenge monopoly positions

### Megafauna Becoming Irrelevant
If players quickly develop the resources to kill every megafauna, the reactive pressure system loses its teeth.

Possible controls:
- The Depth Stalker cannot be permanently killed — only driven off or redirected
- Killing the Shallows Wanderer should have an ecosystem cost significant enough that Ecology actively resists it
- The Contained is explicitly not removable through conventional means

## Next Design Steps

### Immediate priorities
- **Reactor milestone outpost table** — specify exactly which outposts slow which phase transition, what they require to build, and who can build them. This is the document that makes reactor politics concrete.
- **The Drowned civilisation brief** — a short setting document (parallel to the ecology and engineering briefs) covering what they were, what they built, what they activated, and what is still in the cage. This feeds Tier 4–5 card writing and gives Control its most important narrative tool.
- **Megafauna behaviour rules** — one page per creature: trigger condition, movement pattern, removal options and their costs, and the relationship between the Depth Stalker and the Drowned apex.

### Subsequent steps
- Define specific outpost cards
- Define resource types and conversion chains
- Write role sheets for the 30-player game
- Create example expedition structures
- Define scoring and victory conditions
- Add sample event cards
- Write Control brief for The Contained scenario

## Original playtest pacing notes

These notes describe the earlier playtest configuration and are preserved in full as historical facilitation context. The current tutorial route is in Core Rules.

Turn Pacing — 3 players, 2 turns

Playtest configuration: 3 players, all Engineering, 2 turns.

TURN 1 (single shared expedition):
- All 3 players start at Crash Flats.
- One player takes Fabrication Lead (card 3, full role with private brief).
- One player takes the Power Systems Engineer Proxy from the Proxy Roles sheet.
- The third player plays without a role card this playtest — they will gain a Knowledge or Salvage hand card during play.
- Auto-draw cards 1-9 (narrative, dept, role select, phase ref, outpost). Auto-draw the ecosystem setup cards 10-12. Shuffle the remainder.
- Exploration: the team should comfortably draw cards 13 (Salvage Manifest), 14 (The Power Question), and 15 (Engine Core Section). Flipping card 15 requires 2 Oxygen + 1 Power — covered by Oxygen Tank (after upkeep), Fabrication Lead's Power, and Proxy's Power.
- Upkeep: craft 1-2 Aphelion Salvage items. Oxygen Tank (1 Materials + 1 Water) and Field Welder (2 Materials) are achievable from the starting Materials Reserve (3) plus the Coral Shelf / Wreckage Field tracks. If the team flipped Engine Core Section, the Thermal Power Generator becomes craftable on turn 2.

TURN 2 (two parallel expeditions, 3 players splitting 1+2):
- Expedition A (1 player, Reef Gardens): the Lead Ecologist or Knowledge-holding player heads to Reef Gardens. They auto-draw cards 1-12 to set up the location, take the Lead Ecologist/Systems Ecologist role from the deck, then explore cards 13-15. Reef Caverns (card 15) flip requires 2 Oxygen + 1 Knowledge — covered by Lead Ecologist (Oxygen) + Systems Ecologist hand version + Oxygen Tank if needed.
- Expedition B (2 players, Kelp Cathedral): both players carry hand cards from turn 1 (role + 1 salvage each). Entry requirement is 2 Oxygen, satisfied by Oxygen Tank + Lungweed/Lure Fish triggers. They explore the 10-card opening sequence.
- Crisis: Control plays The Aphelion Wraith (CH-1) at the end of turn 2 if any Megafauna Territory token has been placed (Watcher in the Dark in the Reef Caverns sub-deck is the most likely setup). Reactor Pulse (CH-2) is the secondary risk if option A was chosen in The Power Question.

WHY THIS PACES IN 2 TURNS:
- Crash Flats has only 11 actual cards (4 role slots deferred). Drawing all 11 in one turn is achievable if the team prioritises card 15 as their final flip.
- Reef Gardens with 1 player has 22 cards but most are auto-draws (1-12) and the rest are a normal 3-card exploration into cards 13-15. The 7-card Reef Cavern sub-deck is the player's actual content.
- Kelp Cathedral's 10 cards are an opening sequence — 6 of them auto-chain via 'Next:' instructions, leaving 4 cards in the shuffled pile to explore.
- 1 engineering proxy card (Power Systems Engineer) ensures the team always meets Power-icon gates without needing to wait for Fabrication Lead to be in two places at once.
