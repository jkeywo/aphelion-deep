# The Aphelion Deep Departments

Consolidated from the supplied Aphelion Deep design sources. Working design; unresolved passages retain their original status. The tutorial amendments in Core Rules govern the declared three turn slice.

## Departments

Departments define what players can do. They are public, operational structures. Philosophy is a separate hidden layer.

Departments vary in size and effectiveness. Each department should have a leader.

### Command (5 players)
**Function:** Coordination, priorities, and global decision-making

**Strengths:**
- Best overall information
- Can allocate shared resources
- Can direct major strategic decisions
- Has influence over the whole board

**Weaknesses:**
- Limited direct production
- Depends on compliance from other departments

**Roles:**
- Mission Director (Leader)
- Operations Officer
- Communications Officer
- Logistics Controller
- Navigator

### Engineering (5 players)
**Function:** Building, repair, infrastructure, reactor interaction

**Strengths:**
- Primary department for repair and construction
- Controls power, fabrication, and structural systems
- Unlocks advanced modules and technical progression

**Weaknesses:**
- Resource intensive
- Dependent on exploration for materials

**Roles:**
- Chief Engineer (Leader)
- Reactor Specialist
- Fabrication Lead
- Power Systems Engineer
- Structural Engineer

### Exploration (5 players)
**Function:** Mapping, discovery, risk-taking, expedition leadership

**Strengths:**
- Reveals the map
- Finds rare resources and new sites
- Has the most freedom of movement

**Weaknesses:**
- High exposure to danger
- Limited long-term infrastructure contribution

**Roles:**
- Expedition Leader (Leader)
- Deep Dive Specialist
- Surveyor
- Xenobiologist
- Salvage Operator

### Ecology / Permaculture (5 players)
**Function:** Sustainability, food, oxygen, closed-loop systems

**Strengths:**
- Enables long-term survival
- Can reduce resource drain
- Stabilizes and improves outpost efficiency
- Shapes sustainable production loops

**Weaknesses:**
- Slow to start
- Needs space, stability, and inputs

**Roles:**
- Lead Ecologist (Leader)
- Systems Ecologist
- Agronomist
- Water Systems Specialist
- Waste/Recycling Engineer

### Medical / Life Support (5 players)
**Function:** Health, resilience, crisis mitigation

**Strengths:**
- Reduces player losses and penalties
- Can stabilize failing systems temporarily
- Supports dangerous expeditions and harsh outposts

**Weaknesses:**
- Reactive rather than proactive
- Dependent on resources and logistics

**Roles:**
- Chief Medical Officer (Leader)
- Trauma Specialist
- Environmental Health Officer
- Psych Officer
- Med Tech

### Corporate / Data (5 players)
**Function:** Information control, data extraction, and off-world interests

**Strengths:**
- Converts discoveries into value
- Gains advantages from scanning and sampling
- Can influence command indirectly

**Weaknesses:**
- Often misaligned with survival priorities
- Limited direct infrastructure capability

**Roles:**
- Corporate Liaison (Leader)
- Data Analyst
- Xenotech Specialist
- Comms Interceptor
- Asset Manager

## Combined Role Model for a 30 Player Game

Each player has:
- A public department role
- A secret philosophy

This creates mixed loyalties and hidden alliances.

## Role and Philosophy Interactions

The combination of public department and hidden philosophy is the main source of tension.

Examples:
- A Preservationist Engineer may resist reactor repair if it damages the environment
- An Exploiter Ecologist may build sustainable-looking systems only to strip them later
- A Settler in Command may favor stable colonies over direct escape
- A Restorationist in Exploration may prioritize deep reactor access over valuable salvage

This should create discovery, bargaining, and possible deception.
