# Abyssfall — Ecology Department Briefs

---

## The Situation

The *Meridian* is gone. Three days ago it hit the upper atmosphere of an ocean world nobody had a name for, broke apart on the way down, and sank in pieces. You survived. Most of the crew survived. The reactor did not — it's somewhere deep below you, leaking, and the clock is already running.

You are alive on an alien planet with no resupply coming. Everything you need — food, water, oxygen, materials — has to come from this world. Everything you take from this world comes at a cost. Your job is to make that relationship sustainable, or at least survivable.

The **Ecology department** is responsible for keeping people fed, watered, and breathing. Without you, everyone else's plans become irrelevant very quickly. You work from the **Hydroponics Bay** — a ship fragment that's already producing, barely, if someone tends it.

Every player also carries a **philosophy**: a deeper view of what should happen here. Some of you want to leave. Some want to stay. Some want to protect this world. Some want to use it. You don't know which of the others around you shares your view. That's something you'll have to figure out.

---

## Lead Ecologist
*Head of the Ecology Department | Philosophy: Preservationist*

You are the department head. Five people report to you, and roughly thirty depend on what your department produces.

Your public job is coordination: prioritising which outposts get ecological support, deciding where to invest the department's limited energy, and translating biological findings into strategic decisions Command can act on. You sit at the intersection of science, logistics, and survival, and you are one of the few people who understands how fragile all of it is.

Your private view is that this planet is not just a resource. The reef systems here, the kelp forests, the deep-water biology — they are extraordinary. Exploitation will destroy them. You want the group to survive, but not at any price.

**Goals**
- Establish at least two stable ecological systems before the midgame — not just producing, but self-sustaining.
- Prevent or reverse at least one instance of significant overharvesting or ecological damage.
- Identify and protect one high-biodiversity site from outpost construction or industrial use.
- If the group must leave, ensure the planet is left in a condition close to how you found it.

**Guidance**
- Your department is slow to start but compounds. Push for patience and investment early; the payoff comes in the mid-game.
- You have a strong reason to resist the Exploiters, but don't reveal that too quickly — work through ecology first, philosophy second.
- The Ecologists and Water Specialist are natural allies. The Recycling Engineer may not share your values, even though their role looks similar.
- Watch what Engineering and Exploration are actually doing, not just what they say they're doing.

## Systems Ecologist
*Ecology Department | Philosophy: Settler*

You are the department's theorist. You model how systems connect — how the oxygen-producing mats feed into the water cycle, how the soil analogues interact with heat vents, how overharvesting one layer cascades through the rest. If the Lead Ecologist asks "what should we do," you are the one who says "here's what happens if we do each option."

You want to stay. Not because you can't face the journey home, but because you think this world is liveable — and that a functioning colony here would be remarkable. You're not opposed to leaving; you're opposed to leaving before you've found out whether staying was possible.

**Goals**
- Map at least three resource interdependencies on the game map — formally document how one resource feeds another.
- Advise the Lead Ecologist on at least two strategic outpost decisions, with your reasoning recorded.
- Support the development of one closed-loop system that requires no ongoing external input.
- Keep settlement as a viable option alive until at least the late game.

**Guidance**
- You are more useful to Command if they trust your analysis. Make sure they see your work, not just your conclusions.
- Closed-loop systems take setup time. Stake out the space early — good sites fill up.
- Other Settlers are scattered across the departments. The Agronomist may well share your view.
- Don't let the Restorationists dismiss long-term settlement as giving up. It isn't.

## Agronomist
*Ecology Department | Philosophy: Settler*

You grow things. That's not a simple skill here — the "soil" is a mineral analogue, the light is wrong, and the organisms you're working with are alien — but the principles hold. Water, nutrients, growth cycles, yield. You understand those. Right now you're keeping the Hydroponics Bay running on intuition and improvisation. You need better inputs and more space.

You want a colony that works. A real one, long-term. You think the answer to the reactor problem might just be: build something here worth staying for.

**Goals**
- Increase food production output by at least one step before the midgame.
- Successfully cultivate at least one alien species for reliable yield.
- Establish a growing site at a second outpost location.
- Demonstrate that a permanent colony here could be nutritionally self-sufficient.

**Guidance**
- You need Exploration to bring you specimens and site data. Build that relationship early.
- The Water Specialist is your closest working partner. Their outputs are your inputs.
- Don't try to grow everything. Prioritise calorie density first, then variety.
- If someone tries to harvest a site you've designated for cultivation, escalate to the Lead Ecologist immediately.

## Water Systems Specialist
*Ecology Department | Philosophy: Preservationist*

Water here is not scarce, exactly — you're on an ocean planet. But clean, usable water is a different problem. You manage filtration, recycling, and the closed loop that keeps the crew's water supply from becoming a catastrophic liability. If the loop breaks, people get sick. If it breaks badly enough, people die.

You share the Lead Ecologist's concern about this world. The aquatic systems here are intricate in ways that your initial readings only hint at. You think the crew is going to want to use the water systems hard, and you want to make sure someone is paying attention to what that costs.

**Goals**
- Establish a functional water recycling loop that covers the crew's basic needs within the first two turns.
- Document any instances where water extraction or contamination damages local ecosystems.
- Identify one aquatic site that warrants protection from industrial outpost development.
- Reduce water waste across the group's outpost network by the midgame.

**Guidance**
- Your systems have to work before they can be optimised. Reliability first, efficiency second.
- If the reactor is leaking into water systems, that is your emergency. Push Engineering for contamination data.
- You and the Lead Ecologist share a philosophy. You don't have to agree on every decision, but it helps to stay aligned on the big ones.
- The Medical department needs clean water as much as you do. That's a natural alliance.

## Waste / Recycling Engineer
*Ecology Department | Philosophy: Exploiter*

Waste is just resources in the wrong place. You close the loop — organic waste becomes compost becomes yield, material waste becomes feedstock becomes parts, heat becomes power. You are the department's pragmatist. You don't much care what philosophy your colleagues hold; what you care about is whether the numbers balance.

You're not a villain. You just think the others are too sentimental about a planet that is, ultimately, a rock full of useful things. You're happy to build sustainable systems. Sustainability and extraction aren't opposites — done right, you can take a great deal from a place and leave it largely intact. The key word is *largely*.

**Goals**
- Establish at least one waste-to-resource conversion chain that actively reduces the group's resource drain.
- Identify and recover at least two material streams that the group is currently discarding.
- Gain access to a mid-depth or deep site with high extraction value before the midgame.
- Maximise the group's total resource throughput — even if that creates friction with the preservation faction.

**Guidance**
- Your philosophy puts you at odds with the Lead Ecologist eventually. Don't accelerate that conflict before you've established yourself as essential.
- The Corporate / Data department likely shares your priorities. Find your allies early.
- You can argue for extraction using ecological language — "managed harvest", "rotation systems", "sustainable yield" — and you'd be partly right. Use that.
- When the endgame pressure builds, the group will want what you've been stockpiling. That's when your position gets much stronger.
