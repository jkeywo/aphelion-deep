# Abyssfall — Engineering Department Briefs

---

## The Situation

The *Meridian* came apart on the way down. Most of the hull is at the bottom. The reactor went with it — somewhere in the deep, leaking heat and radiation into a world that has never seen either. Three days in, you are alive, the crew is alive, and almost nothing else is functioning the way it should.

That is your problem. Yours specifically.

The **Engineering department** is responsible for everything that was built and everything that needs to be built: outposts, power systems, fabrication capacity, vehicle maintenance, and the reactor itself. Without you, the other departments have ideas and no means of executing them. With you, there is at least a chance.

Your base is the **Engine Core Section** — a ship fragment that is battered but intact enough to work from. It gives you fabrication capability and a direct feed of telemetry from the reactor, which is how you know it is worse than Command has told most people.

Every player also carries a **philosophy**: a deeper view of what should happen here. Some of you want to repair the ship and go home. Some want to build something permanent. Some see the planet as a resource. You do not know which of the others around you shares your view. That is something you will have to work out.

---

## Chief Engineer
*Head of the Engineering Department | Philosophy: Restorationist*

You are the department head. Five people report to you directly, and every department on the planet depends on the infrastructure your team builds and maintains.

Your public role is coordination: prioritising where Engineering effort goes, allocating materials across competing projects, and translating technical capability into decisions Command can act on. You are one of the few people who understands the full picture — what is failing, what can be fixed, and what is already gone. That knowledge is a significant kind of power.

Your private view is simpler: fix the reactor, repair the ship, go home. This world is remarkable, but it is not yours. The reactor is a ticking clock, and every turn spent building permanent colony infrastructure is a turn not spent on the thing that actually matters.

**Goals**
- Establish a functional fabrication pipeline before the midgame — raw materials in, usable components out.
- Make direct contact with the reactor and deliver an accurate assessment of its status to Command within the first two turns.
- Advance the reactor repair or containment track at least twice before the endgame begins.
- If the group chooses to leave, ensure Engineering has the capacity to execute that plan.

**Guidance**
- Your authority is real, but it depends on materials you do not control. Build relationships with Exploration early — they find what you need.
- The Reactor Specialist shares your philosophy. You should identify each other quickly and work as a unit on reactor access.
- Command will want Engineering to do everything at once. They cannot have that. Establish priorities early and hold to them.
- The Fabrication Lead has different priorities than you. Watch what they are actually building, not just what they report.

## Reactor Specialist
*Engineering Department | Philosophy: Restorationist*

You know more about the reactor than anyone alive on this planet. That is not a comfortable position to be in.

The reactor's current state is bad and getting worse. You can model the failure curve; you have been doing it in your head since the crash. What you cannot yet do is act on it, because the reactor is deep, the access route is unknown, and you do not have the equipment you need. Yet. Your job is to close that gap as fast as possible.

You want to go home. Not out of fear — out of clarity. The reactor can be stabilised. The ship can, probably, be repaired. These are engineering problems, and engineering problems have solutions. What you cannot tolerate is watching that window close because someone decided a colony was more important.

**Goals**
- Build or acquire the equipment necessary for a deep-site reactor expedition before the midgame.
- Deliver a full reactor status report to Command with actionable options — not just "it's bad."
- Establish at least one direct intervention on the reactor track before Phase 3.
- Keep the repair-and-leave option technically viable for as long as possible, even if the group has not committed to it.

**Guidance**
- Deep Dive Specialist in Exploration is your most important ally outside Engineering. Find them and brief them on what you need.
- Power systems data from the Engine Core Section tells you more than most people realise. Keep reading it, even when it is alarming.
- You will have to go deep yourself at some point. Make sure you have the equipment for it when that moment comes.
- The Chief Engineer shares your view. You do not need to hide that from each other.

## Fabrication Lead
*Engineering Department | Philosophy: Exploiter*

You build things. More precisely: you turn raw materials into the components, modules, and structures that make everything else possible. Outpost upgrades, expedition equipment, power converters, recycling systems — none of it exists without you running the fabrication line.

You want maximum throughput. That is not a philosophy so much as a professional orientation, but in practice it leads you somewhere specific: this planet has extraordinary resources, and the group is barely touching them. The others argue about sustainability and preservation while leaving enormous value in the ground. You think the smarter approach is to extract aggressively now, build fast, and worry about ecological balance once you have enough capacity to make it matter.

You are not reckless. You are efficient. Those are different things.

**Goals**
- Establish a fabrication output of at least two components per turn by the midgame.
- Identify and begin accessing at least one mid-depth mineral site with high Materials yield.
- Build outpost modules for at least three other departments — use that dependency.
- Accumulate a materials reserve significant enough to give you real leverage over late-game decisions.

**Guidance**
- Corporate / Data shares your priorities and will trade information for components. That is a useful relationship.
- The Chief Engineer will want to direct fabrication capacity toward the reactor. You do not have to refuse — just make sure your own projects stay funded.
- "Managed harvest" and "efficient extraction" are accurate descriptions of what you do. Use that language. It is not dishonest.
- When resources get tight in the late game, everyone will want what you have stockpiled. That is when your position gets much stronger.

## Power Systems Engineer
*Engineering Department | Philosophy: Settler*

Power is the invisible infrastructure. Without it, outposts do not run, fabrication stops, life support degrades, and deep expeditions become impossible. Your job is to make sure it flows — reliably, efficiently, and with enough redundancy that a single failure does not cascade.

You want to stay. Not permanently, perhaps, but long enough to prove it is possible. A functioning colony needs a functioning power grid, and you are the person who knows how to build one. You have been mentally designing it since the first night — the thermal vents in the mid-shelf, the bioluminescent conversion arrays that the biologists keep treating as curiosities, the distributed network that means no single failure point. It is all solvable. The only question is whether anyone gives you the time to solve it.

**Goals**
- Establish a stable base power supply for the Engine Core Section and at least one other outpost before the midgame.
- Connect at least two outposts into a shared power network.
- Identify a deep-tier power source — thermal vent, alien system, or equivalent — and begin working toward it.
- Demonstrate that a permanent colony's power requirements are technically achievable.

**Guidance**
- Ecology's thermal vent data is essential to your long-term plans. Build that relationship early.
- Every outpost upgrade has a power cost. Make sure Engineering is consulted before other departments promise capabilities they cannot power.
- The Structural Engineer shares your philosophy. You will naturally work well together — coordinate on outpost expansion plans.
- Power infrastructure takes time to pay off. Push back against short-term thinking from Command when you can.

## Structural Engineer
*Engineering Department | Philosophy: Settler*

You build the bones. Outpost foundations, pressure housing for deep sites, load-bearing frames, flood barriers, dome supports — the structures that make everything else possible. You are not glamorous, and the work is slow, but without it the colony is a collection of wishes.

You want what the planet is offering: a second chance. Not for the mission — for the species. You have read enough history to know what happens when a civilisation puts all its eggs in one basket. This world is harsh, but it is habitable. A colony here, properly built, could matter enormously. You are not willing to give that up without a fight.

**Goals**
- Establish structural housing at two outpost locations, enabling them to host additional players during upkeep.
- Build or upgrade at least one deep-site outpost with full pressure housing before the endgame.
- Prevent at least one outpost from being abandoned due to structural failure.
- Make the case, with evidence, that a permanent colony here is structurally feasible.

**Guidance**
- Deep outposts need pressure housing that you build. That gives you significant leverage over where expansion goes. Use it.
- The Power Systems Engineer shares your view. Plan outpost expansion together — coordinated builds are more efficient than competing requests.
- Exploration will find sites faster than you can build on them. Identify your priority sites early and claim them before someone else makes a decision for you.
- The Settler philosophy is not about giving up on leaving — it is about making staying viable. Keep framing it that way.
